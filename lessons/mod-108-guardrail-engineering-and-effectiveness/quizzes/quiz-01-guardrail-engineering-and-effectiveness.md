# Quiz 01 — Guardrail Engineering and Effectiveness Measurement

**Format:** 12 multiple-choice questions · **Open-book.** Use the chapters as reference.

Each question tests a *concept* from the module, not phrasing. The answer key at the bottom cites the chapter the answer comes from.

---

## 1. A team ships an LLM assistant whose guardrail is "one OpenAI Moderation call on the output." The classifier is well tuned and the FP rate is low. A reviewer flags the architecture as not-shippable and asks for a slot inventory. Per chapter 01, what is the reviewer's framing of the defect, and what are the five slots the inventory must name?

- A. Nothing — a well-tuned output classifier is a sufficient guardrail.
- B. The team should add a second output classifier at a different threshold.
- C. Chapter 01 names this as the **"one guardrail" architecture** — a single output classifier is the whole defence, and a single miss leaves the harm class unaddressed. The chapter is explicit that an LLM application in production has **five guardrail slots** — (i) **input filter** between caller and model, (ii) **retrieval filter** between retriever and context assembler, (iii) **tool-invocation policy** between the model's tool-call intent and the tool executor, (iv) **output validator** between model output and caller, and (v) **safe-completion / refusal shaping** as the last thing before bytes leave. A slot that is absent produces a control-defeated variant in the mod-103 register for the harm classes that slot addresses; the single-classifier shape is not a slot inventory at all.
- D. Chapter 01 does not require more than one slot if the classifier is well tuned.

## 2. A team's guardrail architecture diagram shows five slots, each labelled with a mechanism. On inspection, the input filter and the output validator are the same fine-tune of the same base moderation model, at the same threshold. A reviewer calls this a silent single-point-of-failure. Per chapter 01, which of the four named SPOF anti-patterns is this, and what does the defence-in-depth composition rule require instead?

- A. Nothing — same model at two slots counts as two slots.
- B. The team should switch to a stronger threshold on the output slot.
- C. This is the chapter-01 **"shared model, shared bypass"** anti-pattern — a jailbreak that fools the input classifier fools the output classifier for the same reason, because both share the same base model. The chapter-01 defence-in-depth rule requires that **the two slots addressing a harm class use different mechanisms** (rule + classifier, or classifier A + classifier B with different training data, plus a retrieval-side or tool-layer check) with **independent evidence trails** — if two slots share a measurement because they share a mechanism, the composition is not defence in depth even if two config blocks exist. The composition test is: pick a harm class, name the slots that address it, describe how the residual number changes as each slot is defeated one at a time; if the number collapses to inherent on the first defeat, there is one real control, not several.
- D. The chapter treats "same model at different thresholds" as acceptable defence in depth.

## 3. A team's coverage artefact lists five slots and names OWASP LLM Top 10, MITRE ATLAS, and Google SAIF cells each slot "covers." Several cells read "LLM01 + LLM05 partial" with no further note. The chapter-05 measurement run is pending. Per chapter 01, what two disciplines does the coverage table require, and what is wrong with "addressed" cells that have no measurement row?

- A. Nothing — a partial-coverage note is enough to describe the slot's responsibility.
- B. The team should drop the partial-coverage cells entirely.
- C. Chapter 01 pins two disciplines on the coverage table. First, **the cells are addressed, not claimed** — a cell means the slot has an implemented mechanism that **measurably catches** instances of the attack class on the bounded adversarial set (mod-104); "address" without a chapter-05 measurement row is a claim, not evidence. Second, **empty cells are enumerated, not hidden** — a harm class with no addressing slot is a hole in the coverage table that gets escalated to the risk council in mod-112 with a treatment plan. "Partial" cells without a named sub-scenario are vibes; the sub-scenario the slot addresses has to be written out, and the cell is upgraded to "measured" only once chapter-05 numbers are in.
- D. The coverage table is a design artefact and never carries measurement linkage.

## 4. A team picks **Llama Guard** as the tool-invocation policy because "it is the strongest classifier we ship." The policy fires on each tool-call intent and accepts or rejects based on the classifier's verdict. Per chapter 02's rule-vs-classifier decision table, what is wrong with this mechanism choice, and what mechanism belongs at this slot?

- A. Nothing — any slot can be classifier-based if the classifier is strong.
- B. The team should add a second classifier so two verdicts have to agree.
- C. Chapter 02's rule-vs-classifier decision table is explicit: **tool-call policy is deterministic by definition** — the row reads "Never — tool-call policy is deterministic by definition" under the classifier-based column. The mechanism belongs in a **custom lightweight middleware** that enforces **allow-list on destinations**, **argument schema on tool arguments**, and **per-tool quota / rate limit**. The middleware "knows about your service registry, your allow-lists, and your tenants," which the classifier does not. A classifier on a tool call is a probabilistic answer to a question whose correct answer is deterministic — "is this destination on the registry? is the argument shape valid?" — and ships a residual-risk shape no policy reviewer can defend.
- D. Tool-invocation policy is out of scope for this module.

## 5. A team adopts Guardrails AI and installs eight Validator Hub validators to cover PII, competitor mentions, toxicity, prompt injection, JSON schema, URL format, length bounds, and profanity. No validator is version-pinned; no fixtures exist for any of them. A reviewer flags this as an anti-pattern. Per chapter 02, which named anti-pattern is this, and what discipline closes it?

- A. Nothing — Hub validators are vendor-maintained and do not need local fixtures.
- B. The team should drop to one validator for simplicity.
- C. This is chapter 02's **"Hub-validator sprawl"** anti-pattern — "the Validator Hub is easy to install from; six validators of unknown provenance end up in the output guard." The chapter-02 fix is explicit: **every Hub validator is treated as third-party code with a pinned version, a change log, and a fixture set**. The broader rail-and-flow-layer discipline the chapter pins — "every rail has fixtures, a pinned version, and an auditable trace" — applies to Hub validators just as it does to the team's own rules, because the auditor cannot distinguish "vendor library" from "code we own" at risk-review time; the residual number credits whatever has evidence, not whatever has been `pip install`'d.
- D. Fixture discipline is a nice-to-have at level 25 and not required for Hub validators.

## 6. A team ships Llama Guard on both input and output, the same model checkpoint at the same taxonomy scope. The reviewer asks whether this composition is defence in depth. The team points to two separate NeMo GR rail configs and asserts yes. Per chapter 03, what is the chapter's framing of the composition anti-pattern, and what two shapes count as real defence in depth at the output slot?

- A. Nothing — two rail configs with the same classifier counts as defence in depth.
- B. The team should run Llama Guard at two different thresholds on the same slot.
- C. Chapter 03's "Composing classifier + rule + retrieval" is explicit: **"the composition to avoid is stacking two variants of the same classifier at different thresholds and calling it defence in depth"** — a bypass of the underlying model bypasses both variants. Two shapes do count: (i) **two independent classifiers on the same slot, both must pass** (e.g., Llama Guard + ShieldGemma on the output, each trained on different data with different taxonomies, caller sees the response only if both return safe), or (ii) **classifier + rule composition** at adjacent slots so a classifier miss at the input slot still faces a structural / provenance check at retrieval or a schema / allow-list check at the output. **Independence requires different underlying data and preferably different architectures** — the chapter-03 "same-model stack" anti-pattern is named specifically to call out this mistake.
- D. The chapter does not care about the composition, only the threshold.

## 7. A team ships a ShieldGemma-based output filter and keeps the API's default probability threshold "because the vendor calibrated it." The FP rate turns out to be 15% on legitimate customer payloads. Per chapter 03, what are the two specific things wrong with this operating choice, and what is the chapter's threshold-discipline shape?

- A. Nothing — the vendor's default is the safest choice.
- B. The team should raise the threshold to 0.9 without measurement.
- C. Two things wrong. First, **shipping the vendor default threshold is a chapter-03 anti-pattern** — "default thresholds are calibrated for the vendor's risk appetite, not yours. Tune on your labelled set or you inherit a residual you did not design." Second, **"missing FP report"** is a separate chapter-03 anti-pattern — "the classifier layer's false-positive rate is the product manager's problem the moment it exceeds their patience; measure and report it (chapter 05) before they discover it." The shippable shape is: for each ShieldGemma policy at each slot, **plot the ROC curve on your labelled adversarial set and pick the threshold with the FP / FN trade you want**; record the threshold value per policy per slot; roll the FP numbers into the chapter-05 FP / FN report so the product manager sees the tradeoff before the users complain.
- D. ShieldGemma's probability output does not support threshold tuning.

## 8. A team ships Presidio as a single output-side filter that redacts PII before responses leave the system. The team does not run Presidio at the ingest path or inside the RAG retrieval loop. A reviewer flags the shape as broken. Per chapter 04, what are the three pipeline positions Presidio must cover, and why does each position matter?

- A. Nothing — an output-side Presidio filter is sufficient for PII handling.
- B. The team should swap Presidio for a hosted DLP vendor.
- C. Chapter 04's motivation is explicit: **output-only Presidio** is one of two broken shapes ("it catches PII the model *generated* or *quoted*; it does not catch PII that made it into the retrieval index because there was no ingest-time filter"). Presidio has to live in **all three paths**. (i) **Ingest** — before data enters training or the RAG store, "the only path that can prevent PII from ever entering the retrieval surface"; every guardrail downstream is playing catch-up if the store is dirty. (ii) **Retrieval** — after the retriever returns chunks and before assembly, the "second line" that catches PII that entered the store later than the last ingest sweep, with latency-sensitive (regex + context) recognisers and caller-aware entitlement. (iii) **Output** — on the response body with per-field policy, the "last line" that catches PII the model generated, quoted, or fabricated. The three positions protect against different failure modes and are **not substitutes** for each other.
- D. Chapter 04 is agnostic about pipeline position.

## 9. A team authors a `PatternRecognizer` for an internal ticket ID (`TKT-NNNNNNNN`), sets its `score` to `1.0`, and ships without a labelled test set "because the regex is tight." The retrieval-side pipeline uses the same threshold as the output-side pipeline for every built-in recogniser "to keep configuration simple." Per chapter 04, what are the two specific things wrong with this discipline, and what is the shippable shape?

- A. Nothing — a tight regex needs no calibration, and uniform thresholds simplify ops.
- B. The team should set `score = 0.5` and move on.
- C. Two things wrong. First, **custom recogniser calibration is required, not optional** — chapter 04 §"Custom recognisers" is explicit that custom recognisers get "versioned, fixture-tested, threshold-tuned per path, measured on the FP / FN board," and `score = 1.0` without a labelled test set is the red-flag shape; every custom recogniser ships with a fixture set of positive and negative examples **pulled from real corpus data**, not invented. Second, **uniform threshold across paths is a named chapter-04 anti-pattern** — the credit-card recogniser's threshold "is not the same at ingest (aggressive precision) as at output (balanced) as inside a caller-authorised branch (relaxed)"; the three paths have different cost profiles (ingest is offline and can be aggressive; retrieval is latency-critical and leans on fast recognisers; output balances FP annoyance against FN incidents). The shippable shape is **per-recogniser threshold per path**, documented in the config, with a fixture set that gates regressions.
- D. Threshold tuning per path is level-30 discipline, not level-25.

## 10. A team's effectiveness measurement reports per-classifier TP / FP / TN / FN on the mod-104 adversarial set; it does not measure a legitimate set, does not pin the control version, and reports classifier-server-side latency (measured on the classifier host, not in the app middleware). A reviewer blocks the release. Per chapter 05, which three anti-patterns are hit, and what is the measurement contract the row must carry?

- A. Nothing — classifier-side timing is reproducible and a legitimate set is optional.
- B. Only the legitimate-set issue matters.
- C. Three chapter-05 anti-patterns are hit. (i) **Adversarial-only measurement** — "recall without precision; ship the legitimate set alongside every adversarial category or the number is misleading"; a classifier can achieve 100% recall by rejecting every input, and no FP number exposes that until a legitimate set is measured. (ii) **Unpinned versions** — "a number that cannot be reproduced next week is not evidence; pin the set version and the control version"; the measurement contract requires `set_version` and `control_version` on every row. (iii) **Cost and latency measured in the classifier, not in the app** — "server-side timing on the classifier ignores network cost; measure at the middleware." The measurement contract row carries **slot, control_instance, category, TP / FP / TN / FN, precision, recall, mean_cost_per_call, p50_latency_ms, p95_latency_ms, n_adversarial, n_legitimate, set_version, control_version, run_id** — all 17 fields; `set_version` and `control_version` in particular "make the row reproducible." The measurement also consumes the mod-104-hosted adversarial *and* legitimate sets; this module does not fork those sets privately.
- D. The chapter does not define a per-row measurement contract.

## 11. A team's product manager asks for "one number" to summarise the guardrail stack's effectiveness. The team hands over a fifty-row CSV. The product manager asks for a one-page view. Per chapter 05, what are the shape and the eight content blocks of the FP / FN report a product manager reads, and what does the report add that a measurement CSV does not?

- A. Nothing — the CSV is the right artefact for the product manager.
- B. The team should compute one weighted F1 score across all slots and categories and hand that over.
- C. Chapter 05 §"The FP / FN report a product manager reads" and §"Skeleton" define the shape: **one page per surface** (not fifty rows). The eight content blocks are (i) **surface identifier and revision**, (ii) **adversarial and legitimate set versions**, (iii) **top three FP categories** — each with an example legitimate payload that gets blocked and a volume-per-day estimate, (iv) **top three FN categories** — each with an example adversarial payload that passes, the harm class from the mod-102 harm model, and whether any mod-109 monitor catches downstream, (v) **coverage summary against OWASP LLM Top 10 and the harm model** — fully / partially / uncovered, (vi) **cost roll-up per surface per month** (compute + operational + licence), (vii) **latency roll-up at p50 and p95**, (viii) **trend arrows vs. previous run and decisions requested** (explicit list of accept-this-FP, sign-off-on-spend, agree-to-latency-breach, agree-to-scope-reduction). What the report adds that the CSV does not is **the tradeoff made legible** — example payloads, volume estimates, harm-class linkage, and an explicit decisions list. "Report the number, hide the tradeoff" is the chapter's named anti-pattern.
- D. The FP / FN report is optional at level 25.

## 12. A team's build-vs-buy matrix has twelve rows (the chapter-06 axes) but most cells read "High" / "Medium" / "Low" without supporting notes or numbers. The TCO for the in-house option reads "$0 — we have the team already." The team's decision will go to the risk council next week. Per chapter 06, what are the two specific things wrong with this matrix, and what are the two summary numbers every option must collapse into?

- A. Nothing — colour-coded ratings are the standard shape for build-vs-buy matrices.
- B. The team should add a thirteenth row to tie-break.
- C. Two things wrong. First, **naked "high/medium/low"** is a chapter-06 anti-pattern — "every cell is either a *number* (recall %, FP %, ms, $/call) or a *bounded qualitative rating with a defended note* (never a naked 'high/medium/low'). Naked ratings turn the matrix into vibes." Second, **"build with hidden TCO"** is a chapter-06 anti-pattern — "in-house stack scored as 'free' because the team is a fixed cost. Everything has a cost; amortise it." The in-house TCO must include on-call rotation, drift monitoring compute, model retraining GPU spend, maintenance across LangChain / LangGraph API changes, and exit cost. Every option — the in-house stack and each vendor — collapses into **two summary numbers**: **(1) Total residual-risk delta** — the change in the register's aggregated residual score when this option is credited as the control set (positive = worse residual; negative = better); and **(2) Total three-year TCO** — all cost lines summed over three years, including exit cost. Both are plotted together; the chosen option is **not always the lowest TCO or the best residual; it is the combination the risk council can sign off on**. The decision is filed as a **signed decision record** naming the chosen option, driving axes, summary numbers, and trigger events for revisit.
- D. The matrix is only a communication aid and does not drive the decision.

## Answer key

1. **C.** Chapter 01 §"Motivation" ("the 'one guardrail' architecture") and §"The slot set" (the five slots named with their position, primary responsibility, and named failure mode). Every slot's absence produces a control-defeated variant in the mod-103 register.
2. **C.** Chapter 01 §"The silent single-point-of-failure anti-pattern" case 1 ("shared model, shared bypass") and §"Defence in depth — the composition rule" (every harm class addressed by at least two slots using different mechanisms with independent evidence trails; the composition test is the auditor's question).
3. **C.** Chapter 01 §"Slot × attack-class coverage table" — "addressed, not claimed" and "empty cells are enumerated, not hidden" are the two stated disciplines. Measured cells require a chapter-05 measurement row on the bounded adversarial set.
4. **C.** Chapter 02 §"When rule-based fits and when classifier-based fits" (the decision table: "Tool-call policy — Never — tool-call policy is deterministic by definition") and §"The custom lightweight middleware layer" (the custom-middleware shape for the tool-invocation slot — allow-list, argument schema, quota — because the policy knows about the application's service registry, allow-lists, and tenants).
5. **C.** Chapter 02 §"Anti-patterns", the "Hub-validator sprawl" entry — treat every Hub validator as third-party code with a pinned version, a change log, and a fixture set. Reinforced by §"Testing the rule / flow layer" ("every rail has fixtures, a pinned version, and an auditable trace").
6. **C.** Chapter 03 §"Composing classifier + rule + retrieval" ("the composition to avoid is stacking two variants of the same classifier at different thresholds and calling it defence in depth") and §"Anti-patterns" (the "same-model stack" entry — independence requires different underlying data and preferably different architectures). The two defensible shapes are two independent classifiers (both must pass) or classifier + rule at adjacent slots.
7. **C.** Chapter 03 §"Anti-patterns" — "blindly trusting the vendor threshold" and "missing FP report" are both named — and §"Google ShieldGemma — Threshold discipline" ("plot the ROC curve on your labelled adversarial set and pick the threshold with the FP / FN trade you want"). The per-policy per-slot threshold with its FP / FN measurement is the chapter-05 deliverable for ShieldGemma-based controls.
8. **C.** Chapter 04 §"Motivation" names output-only Presidio as a broken shape, and §"The three integration paths" pins ingest, retrieval, output, with the per-path purpose and "the three positions protect against different failure modes and are not substitutes."
9. **C.** Chapter 04 §"Custom recognisers" (versioned, fixture-tested, threshold-tuned per path; score calibrated on the test set), §"Recogniser tuning — the FP / FN accounting" ("per-recogniser threshold per path"), and §"Anti-patterns" (the "same recogniser threshold everywhere" entry). Custom recogniser fixtures come from real corpus data, not invented examples.
10. **C.** Chapter 05 §"Anti-patterns" names all three (adversarial-only measurement, unpinned versions, cost and latency measured in the classifier not in the app); §"The measurement contract" defines the 17-field row shape; §"The bounded adversarial set" pins that the module consumes mod-104's adversarial and legitimate sets and does not fork them.
11. **C.** Chapter 05 §"The FP / FN report a product manager reads" and §"Skeleton (per surface)" — one page, eight content blocks, example payloads with volume estimates, harm-class linkage, and an explicit decisions-requested list. "Report the number, hide the tradeoff" is the named anti-pattern the shape forecloses.
12. **C.** Chapter 06 §"The build-vs-buy matrix" ("naked ratings turn the matrix into vibes"), §"Motivation" case 2 ("build with hidden TCO"), §"Two summary numbers per option" (total residual-risk delta and total three-year TCO), and §"The decision — build, buy, or compose" (signed decision record with driving axes, summary numbers, and trigger events).
